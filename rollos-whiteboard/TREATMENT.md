# RollOS — "The 7:40 on a Tuesday"

**Style:** Whiteboard Explainer (`styles/whiteboard`) · **Length:** 60 s · 1920×1080 · 24 fps
**Language:** English · **Voice:** warm, direct American male · **Loudness:** −14 LUFS
**Audience:** independent roll-off hauler, owner-operator, 1–3 trucks.
**Job of the film:** move him from "software is another bill" to "booking shouldn't need me awake", and land him on `rolloffdumpstersoftware.com`.

---

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A. The lost job, rewound.**
Open on `$450` written huge. The eraser wipes it — that is the job lost to voicemail. The rest of the film is how to stop the eraser. Ends with the same `$450` re-written and circled twice, kept.

**B. The 8 PM clock.**
A wall clock on the board reads 7:40. Run the evening forward: phone rings, gloves on, searcher moves to the next hauler. Rewind, run the same evening again with a booking link. Before/after.

**C. Two haulers, two boards.**
Split board, two pens drawing the same Tuesday — notebook on the left, link on the right. Outcomes diverge. Score uses two copies of one figure at two tempi, phasing apart.

### Chosen: **A**, with the clock from B as a diegetic ticker and C's two-pen duet reserved for the cost beat.

Why A. CRO wants a number in the first three seconds, and `$450` *is* the hook — a hauler knows that number in his body. A gives the film one subject (a single job), one goal (keep it), one turn (the booking stops needing him), and it puts the style's strongest native move — **the eraser rewinding** — exactly on the emotional low. B's before/after costs eight seconds on a reset that A gets for free, and C's split board can't hold legible handwriting at 1080p for a 60 s cut. B and C survive as devices inside A, not as the shape.

**Rejected cliché:** the obvious whiteboard move here is a feature list with ticks, or a rising revenue arrow. Both are in every SaaS explainer, neither is a story, and a list has no low point to rewind from.

## 2. Logline and arc

> A hauler loses a $450 job to his own voicemail, and gets it back by taking himself out of the booking.

- **Setup** (0–20 s) — `$450` exists. It calls at 7:40. He is under a container. The eraser takes it.
- **Turn** (20–28 s) — the diagnosis flips: not too slow, just *required*. "Booking needed you awake."
- **Resolution** (28–53 s) — three minutes of setup, one link, the customer does the asking, money to his own Stripe, driver gets a text. Risk removed: starts at zero, no commission, no contract.
- **Ending** (53–60 s) — the same `$450` re-written in the same spot and circled twice, the pen capped, the domain under it. The board's first mark is also its last.

## 3. Benchmark

**minutephysics** — one sentence, one drawing, simplest geometry that carries the idea.
*Learn:* ruthless one-idea-per-line discipline; a number appears exactly as it is spoken; nothing on the board that the narration does not need.
*Don't take:* its hand, its lettering, its stick-figure vocabulary, its music.

**RSA Animate / Cognitive Media** — the continuous board as a single space.
*Learn:* the camera travels instead of cutting; distance on the board equals distance in the argument; the full-board view as a payoff.
*Don't take:* the drawn hand (this style forbids hands), its caricature figures, its specific compositions.

**Direct-response sales-page ledger** (the two-column cost duel) — for one beat only, 50–53 s.
*Learn:* put the two numbers at the same height and let the gap do the arguing.
*Don't take:* red-arrow-and-starburst styling; no fake urgency, no invented savings figure.

## 4. Colour logic

Three markers, meanings held for the whole film:

| Colour | Hex | Means | Never used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dark ink | `#23262c` | the hauler's world: truck, container, calendar, structure, body text | money, customer |
| Blue | `#2a5cb3` | the customer and the signal: the searcher, the booking, the SMS, process arrows | money |
| Orange accent | `#e0601c` | **money that matters**: `$450`, `$0`, the circle, the CTA | anything structural |

One accent per region. Ghost marks stay neutral grey-blue at 6–9 %. The accent appears on props only as its own marker cap and the hero container magnet.

Board: 8000 × 4500, warm white, ghost marks of a *previous* lesson — a faint hand-drawn week grid and the words "call back?" — which pays off at 20 s when the camera passes them.

## 7. Cue map

**96 BPM, 4/4. Bar = 2.5 s. 24 bars.** Clockwork minimalism — marimba, pizzicato cello, woodblock, glockenspiel — chosen because the enemy in this film is *time*, and a ticking ensemble states that without a word. No piano-and-strings.

| Bars | t | Section | Instrumentation | Music state |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | 0–5 | hook | woodblock alone, quarter notes | bare, a metronome |
| 3–5 | 5–12.5 | agitate | + marimba figure, + pizz on 2 and 4 | building, tempo steady |
| 6 | 12.5–15 | **the stop** | everything out; wall clock only | **silence** (diegetic ticker owns it) |
| 7–8 | 15–20 | the loss | single glockenspiel note per bar, falling | emptied out |
| 9–11 | 20–27.5 | the turn | marimba returns, minor → major third | lifting, key change at bar 9 |
| 12–17 | 27.5–42.5 | mechanism | full ensemble, one instrument added per step | accumulating, the "build" |
| 18–19 | 42.5–47.5 | money | + kalimba on the Stripe arrow | warm, resolved |
| 20–21 | 47.5–52.5 | duel | two marimba copies at 96 and 104 BPM, phasing apart | diverging — then snapping to unison on `$0` |
| 22–23 | 52.5–57.5 | bookend | tutti, the opening woodblock figure returns in major | arrival |
| 24 | 57.5–60 | CTA | glockenspiel tag, two notes | open, unresolved upward |

Cue landings: every subtitle in lands on a beat; the eraser's first contact lands on the downbeat of bar 7; the magnet clack lands on beat 1 of bar 16; the two circles at 56.4 land on beats 3 and 4 of bar 23; the key change sits on the word "slow".

## 9. Subtitle and title design

- Board text: single-line hand (EMS Tech / OFL), written in stroke order. `$450` at ~180 px cap; section labels 44–70 px; the CTA domain at 56 px.
- Burned subtitles: off-white rounded label at 90 %, Architects Daughter ~44 px, dark `#23262c` text, a short `#e0601c` marker dash at the left edge. Split at clauses, ≤ 44 characters per line, held ≥ max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). Bottom 12 % of frame kept clear of board content in every shot.
- A word finishes writing exactly on the narrator's word: `$450`, "awake", "3 min", "100%", `$0`.
- Exported as `rollos-whiteboard.srt` alongside the film.

## 10. Honesty constraints (CRO without a claim problem)

The user asked to drop statistics, so **no third-party figures appear**. Every number on the board is either the user's own published pricing or a plainly hypothetical job value:

- `$450` — framed as "a job", not as an average or a study. The site uses the same figure in the same way.
- `$0 / month` — spoken as "**starts at** zero a month", true of the published Free tier.
- `$150–350` — the competitor band already published in the user's own comparison table, labelled "legacy software" and not attributed to any named company on screen.
- "You keep the rental" / `100%` — the published 0 % hauler commission.
- The $12 customer booking fee is **not hidden**: 44.2 s says the customer covers the software, which is what makes the zero honest.
- No invented testimonial, no fake logos, no "haulers trust us" count, no urgency device.

## 11. What changed between this treatment and the built film

The treatment above is the plan as written before any drawing. These are the decisions that
changed during the build, and why. Nothing in the story, the arc or the claim set moved.

1. **A full-board view was added as shot 15** (52.95–54.0 s), between the price duel and the
   bookend. The planned cut went straight from the duel on the far right of the board back to
   the money region on the far left — about 6,500 world units in a second and a half, which
   smeared into an unreadable whip. Pulling back to the whole board first solves the traverse
   *and* pays off the style's "the whole lesson as one picture" move. The ending is unchanged.

2. **The board grew from 8000 × 4500 to 11000 × 5600** and the regions were spread further
   apart. At the original spacing three shots clipped a neighbouring region's label at the frame
   edge — the duel showed the tail of "booking needed you", the ticks shot cut "3 min" — which
   breaks the rule that a label is fully in or fully out of every shot.

3. **The accent colour means "the number that makes the case", not strictly money.** The
   treatment's table reserved orange for money alone, which left the setup region with no accent
   and reading flat. Orange now also carries `3 min`. Blue and black are unchanged, and orange
   still never appears on structure.

4. **"7:40" is written on the board next to the clock.** At 7:40 the hour and minute hands very
   nearly overlap, so the face alone cannot be read — correct, and useless. The time is now named
   in the same hand as the narrator says it.

5. **The $12 customer booking fee is not in the film.** The plan (§10) was to disclose it at
   44.2 s. At 60 seconds there is nowhere to put it legibly: a 30-character line needs about
   3.5 s of held screen time to be readable, and every shot in the back half is already carrying
   its own content. Rather than flash it too briefly to read — which is worse than not showing it —
   it was cut, **and the claims were narrowed to match**: the narration says "Zero a month to
   start. No contract.", which is a statement about the subscription and is true of the published
   Free tier. The film makes no claim that there are no fees anywhere. This is the one item worth
   a decision from the client: see the delivery notes.

6. **Word timings come from the audio, not from a transcript.** `tools/asr_check.py` needs a
   Whisper model from Hugging Face, which this environment's network policy blocks, so the
   speech-to-text self-check could not run. `tools/gen_words.py` replaces it: it measures each
   line's real silence gaps, snaps the clause boundaries to them, and interpolates only the
   within-clause onsets. The boundaries a viewer notices are measured; the rest lands within a
   frame or two at 24 fps.
